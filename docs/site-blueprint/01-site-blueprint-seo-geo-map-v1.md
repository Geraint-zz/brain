brain is an English-language, global, evidence-informed brain health and cognitive training platform. It combines practical public education with neutral product evaluation and industry research. It serves students, knowledge workers, older adults, education providers, employers, brain-health brands, and investors.

## Expansion boundary

Only the mapped 20 posts and their genuine hubs are live in v1. New Categories require at least two approved posts. Clinical diagnosis, treatment, disease-prevention, medication/supplement efficacy, and personalized medical guidance are out of scope unless medical-review, sources, and legal rules are approved. Review/recommendation pages require method, evidence, update date, and any affiliate disclosure. Industry claims require verifiable underlying research or interviews.

## SEO/GEO authority

Site topic: evidence-informed brain health and cognitive training. Primary clusters: practical focus/memory/sleep guidance; audience-specific cognitive routines; neutral tools and training reviews; cognitive-training evidence; digital brain-health market and program evaluation.

Every live page owns one primary query, declares one primary intent, and uses only content-supported claims. Categories answer broader discovery questions; Posts answer discrete practical or evaluative questions. Each Category and Post evaluates a direct answer, answer type, evidence limitations, entities, and a professional-help trigger. FAQ is optional and only permitted when the body supports it.

Internal links: Homepage → Section → Category → Post; each Post links to its parent Category and contextually relevant Posts. Cross-section links are editorially justified only. Canonical host is pending and must be set before launch.

## Trust and editorial rules

Health and product-effect claims use calibrated language (for example, “evidence suggests” or “may”) and citations. Pages show updated dates only when later than publication. Do not invent authors, credentials, team details, testing, affiliations, or partners. Research and review pages require source/methodology review; public guides require health-safety and source review.
